Why a Dedicated SIF Triage System?

Most safety programs treat all incidents equally. DEKRA & EEI research shows only 20–25% carry genuine fatal potential.

Traditional Approach

Manual Periodic Safety Review

  • 30–90 day reporting lag: Observations collated manually on monthly or quarterly intervals, delaying corrective action.
  • Injury distortion effect: High-volume low-severity incidents (slips, sprains) overshadow zero-injury fatal precursors in prioritisation.
  • No standard taxonomy: Free-text field slips lack consistent hazard classification, making cross-site benchmarking unreliable.
This Platform

Automated SIF Triage & Classification

  • Immediate classification: Incident reports are assessed and risk-rated as soon as they are submitted — no batch processing.
  • Energy & barrier analysis: Separates high-energy hazard sources (E) and barrier integrity state (B) from minor housekeeping observations.
  • Auto IOGP & OISD mapping: Each report is automatically mapped to the relevant Life-Saving Rule and corresponding OISD action plan.

Classification Methodology — Energy & Barrier Analysis

Based on DEKRA & EEI industry research. A SIF precursor requires both a high-energy hazard source and a compromised or missing control barrier.

Step 1 — Energy Source Assessment

High-Energy Hazard Identification

The report is scanned for physical energy thresholds with potential to cause serious injury or fatality:

Pressure: > 100 PSI
Working at Height: > 2.0 m
Suspended Load: > 500 kg
Electrical: > 50 V
Flammables: H₂S & Crude / LPG
Mobile Heavy Equipment
Step 2 — Barrier Integrity Check

Control Barrier Status Evaluation

Determines whether engineered or procedural barriers were present and functioning:

LOTO / Isolation: BOP & Valve
Fall Protection: Rig Harness
Pressure Relief Valves
Gas Monitoring Alarms
Whip-Check Restraints
Permit to Work (PTW)

Industry Benchmark — Why Fatal and Non-Fatal Incidents Have Different Root Causes

DEKRA & EEI 15-year analysis of US industrial accidents shows that low-severity incident rates and fatality rates are decoupled — separate intervention strategies are required.

Non-Fatal Incident Rate Reduction (15 years) −51%
Driven by improved ergonomics and PPE compliance — effective for slips, strains, and minor injuries.
Fatal Incident Rate Reduction (same 15 years) −25.5%
Fatalities declined at half the rate — because fatal precursors require dedicated energy-barrier controls, not PPE.
